Why We Focus on Mice, Not Every Pest

We work on mice and rats, not every bug that shows up in a Truckee house. That focus means we know how a house mouse moves through a kitchen and how a deer mouse gets into an attic, instead of treating every rodent the same way a general exterminator would.

House Mice and Deer Mice in Truckee

Two kinds of mice cause almost every call in this area, and they don't act the same way.

Mouse Type Where It's Found What Gives It Away
House Mouse Kitchens, pantries, and garages, close to food Small droppings near food storage, gnaw marks on packaging
Deer Mouse Attics, wall voids, and storage areas Droppings in the attic, nests made from shredded paper or insulation, and a higher hantavirus risk

House mice stay close to food and water, so they rarely travel more than a few feet from their nest. Deer mice climb well and often end up higher in a house, in the attic or behind stored boxes.

How Do I Know If It's Mice and Not Rats?

Mouse droppings are small, about the size of a grain of rice, while rat droppings are larger and rounder. A mouse can also fit through a gap the size of a quarter inch, while a rat needs a gap closer to the size of a coin.

Why Mice Get Into Truckee Homes After the First Freeze

Cold weather pushes mice indoors looking for food and warmth, and a Truckee winter gives them plenty of chances to get in. According to the University of Kentucky Extension, house mice are especially likely to move indoors during their fall migration in search of warmth, food, and shelter. Once inside, a mouse doesn't travel far. It usually stays within a few feet of its nest, which is why one mouse in a pantry often means a nest somewhere close by, not just a single visitor passing through.

Attics, Crawlspaces & Kitchen Cabinet Intrusions

Mice frequent food storage zones, lower kitchen baseboards, subfloor plumbing dropouts, and insulation gaps in crawlspaces. They easily climb wall framing to access attic voids.

We trace entry points around sink pipes, dishwasher openings, garage utility doors, and crawlspace vents to block hidden highways.

Vacation Property & Seasonal Home Risk

Mice can multiply rapidly in secondary properties left unattended during off-seasons. A minor gap can become a major nesting infestation, ruining mattresses, linens, and pantry supplies.

We provide long-term sealing, rodent monitoring, and maintenance checks for unoccupied homes.

How We Remove Mice

Mice control takes more than a few traps under the sink. A mouse nest left in place keeps producing more mice, since a female can have several litters in a single year, so the work has to find the nest and the entry point, not just the mouse you saw.

We start by checking the kitchen, pantry, attic, and garage for droppings, gnaw marks, and grease trails along walls, since mice tend to travel the same path along an edge every time. Traps get placed along that path, not just near the food source. We skip poison in most homes, since a mouse that dies inside a wall can leave a smell for weeks. Once the mice are gone, we close the gap they used with steel mesh or metal flashing, sized to the actual opening.

How Fast Can Mice Take Over a House?

A single mouse can lead to a much bigger problem quickly, since a female mouse can have several litters in a year with multiple pups each time. What looks like one mouse in October can turn into a real infestation within a few weeks if the entry point never gets sealed.

Is It Worth Hiring a Professional for Mice Control?

A few store bought traps can catch a mouse here and there, but they rarely find the nest or the gap it used to get in. A professional inspection finds the actual source of the problem, which is the part that stops new mice from showing up every few weeks.

Protecting Second Homes and Rentals from Mice

A kitchen or pantry that sits untouched for weeks gives mice an easy, undisturbed food source, which makes a second home near Prosser Lakeview or Donner Lake an easy target between visits. Since mice stay close to their nest, one that settles into a pantry can go unnoticed until an owner arrives and finds droppings or chewed packaging. We take photos of everything we find, so an owner who wasn't there can see exactly what happened and what got fixed.

Signs of a Mice Problem in Truckee

  • Small droppings near food storage, about the size of a grain of rice
  • Gnaw marks on food packaging, boxes, or wiring
  • A musky smell in closets or under sinks
  • Scratching sounds in the walls or attic at night
  • Grease marks along a wall where a mouse repeats the same path

Frequently Asked Questions

Why Do Mice Come Inside During Winter?
Cold weather cuts off the food and shelter mice rely on outdoors, so they move inside looking for both. A house offers steady warmth and easy access to food, which is why sightings usually rise once temperatures drop.
Do Home Remedies Like Peppermint Oil Actually Work?
Peppermint oil and similar smells can make a mouse avoid one specific spot for a short while, but they don't solve the actual problem. Once the smell fades, mice return to the same path unless the entry point gets sealed.
How Small a Gap Can a Mouse Fit Through?
A mouse can fit through a gap about the size of a quarter inch, roughly the width of a pencil. If a gap looks too small to matter, it's still worth checking, since a mouse can squeeze through spaces that look impossible.
Are Mice Dangerous to Health?
Yes. Mice can spread salmonella through droppings and urine, and their gnawing can damage wiring enough to cause a fire risk. Cleanup after a mouse problem should include sanitizing, not just trapping.
Do I Need to Bait the Traps?
Bait helps in most cases, but it isn't always necessary if traps are placed directly on a mouse's travel path along a wall. A technician who knows where mice are actually moving can place traps that work with little or no bait at all.
