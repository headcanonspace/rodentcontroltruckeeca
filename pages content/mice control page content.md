## H2: House Mice and Deer Mice in Truckee

Two kinds of mice cause almost every call in this area, and they don't act the same way.

| Mouse Type | Where It's Found | What Gives It Away |
|---|---|---|
| **House Mouse** | Kitchens, pantries, and garages, close to food | Small droppings near food storage, gnaw marks on packaging |
| **Deer Mouse** | Attics, wall voids, and storage areas | Droppings in the attic, nests made from shredded paper or insulation, and a higher hantavirus risk |

House mice stay close to food and water, so they rarely travel more than a few feet from their nest. Deer mice climb well and often end up higher in a house, in the attic or behind stored boxes.

### H3: How Do I Know If It's Mice and Not Rats?

Mouse droppings are small, about the size of a grain of rice, while rat droppings are larger and rounder. A mouse can also fit through a gap the size of a quarter inch, while a rat needs a gap closer to the size of a coin.

## H2: Why Mice Get Into Truckee Homes After the First Freeze

Cold weather pushes mice indoors looking for food and warmth, and a Truckee winter gives them plenty of chances to get in. Once inside, a mouse doesn't travel far. ## H2: How We Remove Mice

Mice control takes more than a few traps under the sink. A mouse nest left in place keeps producing more mice, since a female can have several litters in a single year, so the work has to find the nest and the entry point, not just the mouse you saw.

We start by checking the kitchen, pantry, attic, and garage for droppings, gnaw marks, and grease trails along walls, since mice tend to travel the same path along an edge every time. Traps get placed along that path, not just near the food source. We skip poison in most homes, since a mouse that dies inside a wall can leave a smell for weeks. Once the mice are gone, we close the gap they used with steel mesh or metal flashing, sized to the actual opening.

### H3: How Fast Can Mice Take Over a House?

A single mouse can lead to a much bigger problem quickly, since a female mouse can have several litters in a year with multiple pups each time. What looks like one mouse in October can turn into a real infestation within a few weeks if the entry point never gets sealed.

## H2: Frequently Asked Questions

### H3: Why Do Mice Come Inside During Winter?

Cold weather cuts off the food and shelter mice rely on outdoors, so they move inside looking for both. A house offers steady warmth and easy access to food, which is why sightings usually rise once temperatures drop.

### H3: Do Home Remedies Like Peppermint Oil Actually Work?

Peppermint oil and similar smells can make a mouse avoid one specific spot for a short while, but they don't solve the actual problem. Once the smell fades, mice return to the same path unless the entry point gets sealed.

### H3: How Small a Gap Can a Mouse Fit Through?

A mouse can fit through a gap about the size of a quarter inch, roughly the width of a pencil. If a gap looks too small to matter, it's still worth checking, since a mouse can squeeze through spaces that look impossible.

### H3: Are Mice Dangerous to Health?

Yes. Mice can spread salmonella through droppings and urine, and their gnawing can damage wiring enough to cause a fire risk. Cleanup after a mouse problem should include sanitizing, not just trapping.

### H3: Do I Need to Bait the Traps?

Bait helps in most cases, but it isn't always necessary if traps are placed directly on a mouse's travel path along a wall. A technician who knows where mice are actually moving can place traps that work with little or no bait at all.
