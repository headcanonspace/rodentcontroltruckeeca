## H2: When Rodents Are Most Active in Truckee

**Cold Season (Late Fall Through Winter)**

Once the ski resorts open and temperatures drop for good, mice and rats start moving out of open fields and into garages, attics, and crawl spaces near the tree line. As snow piles up through the heart of winter, the weight can shift fascia boards and open gaps around soffits. Everything's buried outside, so the first real sign is usually scratching at night rather than anything visible from the ground.

**Warm Season (Spring Through Summer)**

As the snowpack melts, foundation cracks that opened over the winter become visible again, and garage or pantry doors worn down by a season of use turn into new entry points. Once ski season winds down and the town quiets into its spring-to-summer stretch, outdoor rodent activity drops off, making this the best window to check attics and crawl spaces and seal anything up, especially on properties that sit empty for weeks at a time in the off-season.

### H3: How Can I Stop Rodents From Coming Back?

The biggest factor is timing, check your attic and crawl space before the first cold snap, not after you hear something at night. Beyond that, sealing gaps a quarter inch or larger and keeping food in sealed containers goes a long way, but the yearly pre-winter check is what actually catches small gaps before they turn into an open door.

## H2: Signs You May Have a Rodent Problem

1. Small dark droppings in kitchen drawers, along baseboards, or in the garage
2. Chewed insulation, cardboard, or wiring, especially in the attic or engine bay
3. Scratching or scurrying sounds overhead, usually worse after dark

A stale, ammonia-like smell that gets stronger in enclosed spaces like a pantry or closet is also worth paying attention to.

A single dropping here or there with nothing else going on is often an early, small issue. Sounds at multiple times of day, damage in more than one spot, or a smell that keeps coming back usually means it's grown into something bigger.

### H3: How Many Mice Actually Means an Infestation?

There's no exact number, it's more about the pattern than the count. Seeing one mouse in daylight is actually a worse sign than seeing several at night, since mice avoid light and open spaces unless the nest behind the walls is already overcrowded. If you're finding droppings in more than one room, hearing activity at different times of day, or the smell keeps returning after cleaning, it's worth a full inspection rather than a few traps and waiting.
