| Season | Time of Year | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **First Freeze Migration** | October–November | Mice and rats move from open fields toward attics, garages, and crawl spaces, especially near forest edges |
| **Heavy Snow** | December–February | Snow load shifts rooflines and opens soffit and fascia gaps. Snow cover hides exterior signs, so scratching sounds at night are often the first clue |

| **Prevention Window** | May–September | Outdoor activity drops, making this the best time to seal gaps and check insulation before the next freeze, especially in properties left empty for stretches at a time |

### H3: How Can I Stop Rodents From Coming Back?

Seal any gap over a quarter inch with steel mesh, keep food stored in sealed containers, and check your crawl space and attic every fall before the first freeze. A yearly check before winter is the best way to catch small gaps before they turn into entry points.

## H2: Signs You May Have a Rodent Problem

- Droppings near cabinets or in the garage
- Gnaw marks on boxes or wiring
- Scratching sounds in the attic at night
- A persistent smell, which usually means the population has grown past a one trap fix

If you're only seeing one or two droppings and nothing else, it may be an early, small issue. Repeated sightings or sounds at multiple times of day usually mean it's grown into something bigger.

### H3: Do Three Mice Mean an Infestation?

Seeing three mice usually means more are already living somewhere in the house, since mice reproduce quickly and stay hidden during the day. It's worth a full inspection rather than just setting a few traps and waiting.
