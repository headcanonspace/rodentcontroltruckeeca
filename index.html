Why Rodents Get Into Truckee Homes

Truckee sits high up in the mountains with long, cold winters. This changes how rodents act compared to towns that are lower and warmer. Once nights get cold, mice and rats leave open fields and look for someplace warm. Snow sitting on a roof can push up boards and vents just enough to open a small gap. A mouse only needs a gap the size of a quarter inch to get through. Once a rodent gets inside, it leaves a scent trail within two to three days, and that trail brings more rodents in behind it.

This is why a home near Alder Creek or along Donner Pass Road can go from one mouse in October to a real problem by December if the entry point never gets sealed. Trapping alone gets rid of the mice that are already inside, but it does nothing about the gap they came through. Sealing that gap is what actually stops it from happening again next season.

Why Do Rodents Invade Truckee Homes After the First Freeze?

Rodents invade Truckee homes after the first freeze because cold nights push them to look for warmth indoors. Snow weight often lifts roof boards or bends vents around the same time, opening new gaps for them to use. Once inside, a rodent marks a path that other rodents follow all winter.

How Can I Stop Rodents From Coming Back?

Seal any gap over a quarter inch with steel mesh, keep food stored in sealed containers, and check your crawl space and attic every fall before the first freeze. A yearly check before winter is the best way to catch small gaps before they turn into entry points.

Signs You May Have a Rodent Problem

  • Droppings near cabinets or in the garage
  • Gnaw marks on boxes or wiring
  • Scratching sounds in the attic at night
  • A persistent smell, which usually means the population has grown past a one trap fix

If you're only seeing one or two droppings and nothing else, it may be an early, small issue. Repeated sightings or sounds at multiple times of day usually mean it's grown into something bigger.

Do Three Mice Mean an Infestation?

Seeing three mice usually means more are already living somewhere in the house, since mice reproduce quickly and stay hidden during the day. It's worth a full inspection rather than just setting a few traps and waiting.
