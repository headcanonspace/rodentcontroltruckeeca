Rat Control in Downtown Truckee

A rat in an old downtown building usually comes down to two things: the Truckee River running along the edge of town, and how old the foundation is. Rats travel along water for cover and food, so buildings closest to the river tend to see more activity than ones a few streets back. A foundation that has settled for a century or more also has cracks and gaps a newer foundation just doesn't have yet.

How Do I Know If It's a Rat and Not a Mouse in an Older Building?

Rat droppings are bigger, close to the size of a raisin, while mouse droppings are much smaller, closer to the size of a grain of rice. In an older building, rats tend to show up near the foundation or a shared basement, while mice show up higher up, in a pantry or between floors.

Mouse Control in Downtown Truckee

Mice care less about the river and more about food and warmth, which is why a building like the historic Truckee Hotel, built in 1873 with living space above the ground floor, is exactly the layout that mice look for. A pantry on one floor and a quiet gap between the ceiling and the floorboards above it gives a mouse both food and shelter without ever crossing outside. We check both levels in a building like this, not just the unit where the droppings showed up.

Rodent Control for Restaurants and Shops on Commercial Row

Commercial Row has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 2009, and running a restaurant or shop in one of these buildings comes with a different kind of pressure than a home does. A single rodent sighting during a health inspection counts against the score, and pest control is one of the categories inspectors weigh most heavily. We document every visit with photos, so an owner has something to show if a health inspector ever asks what's being done about it.

What Happens If a Health Inspector Finds a Rodent?

A live or dead rodent found during inspection is treated as a critical violation, and it can affect the outcome right away. Having a documented pest control plan in place, not just a sighting handled after the fact, is what inspectors want to see.

Common Rodent Entry Points in Downtown's Older Buildings

Old brick and mortar move a little every year, and after a century and a half of freeze and thaw, that adds up. A building near Jibboom Street, home to the old 1875 jail, shows the same kind of settling as most of downtown's oldest structures: small gaps around window frames, mortar that has thinned with age, and foundation seams that were never sealed the way a modern building's would be. None of this shows up on a walk past the front door. It takes checking the actual structure to find it.

Can You Work Within Historic Building Restrictions?

Yes. Sealing work on a historic building uses materials and methods that don't alter the building's exterior character, since many downtown properties have restrictions on visible changes. We ask about a building's status before starting any exterior work.

What a Trap Alone Can't Fix in a Building This Old

A trap catches the rodent that's already inside, but it does nothing about the gap it used to get there or the next one that finds the same spot. In a building this old, that gap usually isn't in the room where the sighting happened. It's somewhere else in the structure entirely. Sealing that actual entry point is the only part of the job that stops the problem from repeating.

Signs of a Rodent Problem in a Downtown Building

  • Droppings near a shared basement, foundation wall, or storage area
  • Chewed wiring, cardboard, or insulation in a wall void or crawl space
  • Scratching sounds between floors, especially above a shared wall or in an apartment above a business
  • A smell that gets stronger in an enclosed space like a stockroom or pantry

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions about rodent control in older commercial and residential properties in Downtown Truckee.

What Is the Average Cost to Get Rid of Mice?
Cost depends on how far the problem has spread and how much sealing work the building needs. A small, early problem costs less than one that's already settled into wall voids or a shared space between units.
Is It Worth Hiring a Professional, or Can I Handle It Myself?
Store bought traps can catch what's already inside, but they don't find the entry point or fix it. In an older downtown building, finding that gap usually takes checking the structure itself, not just the room where the problem showed up.
Is Hantavirus a Concern Downtown, or Mostly Elsewhere in Truckee?
Hantavirus risk is tied mainly to deer mice, which are more common in outlying areas with more open land than in the middle of downtown. House mice and rats are the more common downtown concern, and they carry different, though still real, health risks.
Why Do We Keep Seeing Mice Despite Regular Pest Control Visits?
If traps get checked and reset but the entry point never gets sealed, a new mouse can use the same gap a previous one used. Regular visits without exclusion work treat the symptom, not the cause.
Do Older Downtown Buildings Need a Different Approach Than Newer Homes?
Yes. Older buildings have settled over many decades, shared walls with neighboring units, and construction materials that a newer home doesn't have, so finding and sealing entry points takes a different kind of inspection than a house built in the last twenty years.
